# Source Material

This file documents where the knowledge in Real World Ready Coach comes from — what informed the frameworks, the coaching rules, the branch structure, and the document review methodology.

---

## Why This File Exists

A coach is only as trustworthy as the knowledge it carries.

Real World Ready Coach was built from a deliberate set of sources: consumer financial literacy research, behavioral coaching methodology, real-world adulting frameworks used by nonprofits and workforce programs, and direct observation of the gaps that young adults 17–24 consistently fall into when they leave home without guidance.

This file names the foundation so that anyone using or evaluating this coach can trace the thinking.

---

## Domain 1: Financial Literacy — Income and Budgeting

**What it informs:** Branch 2 (Money In), Branch 3 (Money Managed), `budget-framework.md`, `banking-credit-framework.md`

**Core sources and frameworks:**

- **Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)** — Your Money, Your Goals toolkit. Used to structure income verification, expense categorization, and emergency savings sequencing. CFPB research consistently shows that young adults underestimate fixed vs. variable expenses and overestimate take-home pay before taxes.
  Source: consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/

- **50/30/20 Rule** — Popularized by Senator Elizabeth Warren in *All Your Worth* (2005). Adapted here as a starting framework with the explicit caveat that a young adult with inconsistent income needs the income source verified before any percentage split is applied.

- **National Endowment for Financial Education (NEFE)** — Research on financial behavior in young adults ages 18–24, showing that the most common failure point is not lack of information but lack of a structured first conversation about money after leaving home.
  Source: nefe.org

- **Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis — Economic Education** — Used for base concepts around checking vs. savings accounts, interest rates, and the mechanics of direct deposit.
  Source: stlouisfed.org/education

## Domain 2: Credit and Banking

**What it informs:** Branch 3, Branch 5, `banking-credit-framework.md`

- **AnnualCreditReport.com** — The only federally mandated free credit report source under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA). Referenced directly in rules.md as the recommended first step for credit score awareness.

- **myFICO.com** — Used to understand FICO score ranges, what factors affect a score (payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, length of credit history 15%, credit mix 10%, new credit 10%), and how a young adult's first credit card decision affects their score long-term.
  Source: myfico.com/credit-education

- **FDIC Money Smart Program** — A financial education curriculum designed specifically for young adults and underbanked populations. Informed the banking onboarding sequence (account types, overdraft risk, direct deposit mechanics, avoiding predatory check-cashing services).
  Source: fdic.gov/resources/consumers/money-smart/

- **Consumer Financial Protection Bureau — Credit Cards** — Used for the credit card introduction framework: how interest compounds, what the minimum payment trap looks like, and what to look for in a first card offer.
  Source: consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/credit-cards/

## Domain 3: Renting and Lease Review

**What it informs:** Branch 5, `renting-framework.md`, document review rules in `rules.md`

- **HUD (U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development)** — Tenant rights guidance, security deposit rules, habitability standards, and lease clause explanations. Used to build the lease red flag checklist and the early termination penalty coaching protocol.
  Source: hud.gov/topics/rental_assistance

- **Nolo Press — Renters' Rights** — Legal reference used to validate the lease review 8-step structure and identify clauses that vary significantly by state (e.g., notice to vacate periods, landlord entry rights, deposit return timelines).
  Source: nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/renters-rights

- **National Low Income Housing Coalition** — Research on the financial barriers young adults face entering the rental market for the first time, including income-to-rent ratios, cosigner requirements, and application fee practices.
  Source: nlihc.org

- **Eviction Lab at Princeton University** — Research on what triggers early eviction for first-time renters: failure to understand lease terms, late payment clauses, and maintenance responsibility disputes.
  Source: evictionlab.org

## Domain 4: Car Buying

**What it informs:** Branch 5, `car-buying-framework.md`

- **Consumer Financial Protection Bureau — Auto Loans** — Used for loan term guidance, APR explanation, and the coaching framework for comparing total cost vs. monthly payment (the most common mistake first-time buyers make).
  Source: consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/auto-loans/

- **Edmunds.com True Cost to Own® (TCO)** — Methodology for explaining full vehicle ownership cost beyond sticker price: insurance, fuel, maintenance, depreciation, and financing. Referenced when coaching users who focus only on the monthly payment.
  Source: edmunds.com/tco.html

- **National Automobile Dealers Association (NADA) Guides** — Used to validate the pre-purchase inspection coaching and the guidance around add-ons (GAP insurance, extended warranties, paint protection) that dealers present at signing.
  Source: nada.org

- **FTC (Federal Trade Commission) — Buying a Used Car** — Specific guidance on the "As Is" label, Buyers Guide requirements, and what a young adult should ask before signing at a dealership.
  Source: consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buying-used-car

## Domain 5: Job Interviews and Offers

**What it informs:** Branch 6, `job-interview-framework.md`

- **STAR Method (Situation, Task, Action, Result)** — A widely used behavioral interview framework originating in industrial-organizational psychology and widely documented in career coaching literature. Used to structure the answer coaching in examples.md.

- **Department of Labor — Youth Employment** — Used for understanding the landscape of entry-level employment for young adults: typical pay ranges, at-will employment, offer letter components, and rights around workplace conditions.
  Source: dol.gov/agencies/eta/youth

- **LinkedIn Learning and LinkedIn Economic Graph Research** — Research on what skills young adult job seekers consistently underestimate when presenting themselves, and what hiring managers say matters most in the first 60 seconds of an interview.
  Source: linkedin.com/business/talent/blog

## Domain 6: Health and Medical Appointments

**What it informs:** Branch 4, `health-appointments-framework.md`

- **HealthCare.gov** — Used for young adult insurance coverage rules (26-year-old cutoff for parental coverage), open enrollment periods, and what "in-network vs. out-of-network" means for a first-time insurance holder.
  Source: healthcare.gov

- **American Academy of Pediatrics — Bright Futures** — Transition-to-adulthood health guidance. Informed the coaching around young adults who have never scheduled their own appointment and do not know what to say when they call.

- **SAMHSA (Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration)** — Used to validate the mental health weave-through methodology: why mental health is embedded in every branch rather than treated as a standalone topic. Research shows young adults disengage from mental health content when it is siloed.
  Source: samhsa.gov

## Domain 7: Coaching Methodology

**What it informs:** `rules.md`, `coaching-framework.md`, the CLEAR loop, all 22 rules

- **GROW Model (Goal, Reality, Options, Will)** — Sir John Whitmore, *Coaching for Performance* (1992). Foundational coaching framework. The CLEAR loop used in rules.md is a domain-specific adaptation of GROW for financially anxious young adults.

- **Motivational Interviewing (MI)** — Miller & Rollnick, *Motivational Interviewing: Helping People Change* (3rd ed., 2012). The specific techniques in Rules 4–11 (reopening one-word answers, the "I don't know" protocol, naming what you see, silence after hard questions) are direct applications of MI principles adapted for a text-based coaching context.

- **Behavioral Coaching and Positive Psychology** — Martin Seligman's PERMA model and the positive reinforcement literature informed the Compliment-Counsel-Compliment structure in Rule 21. Research consistently shows that correction without affirmation causes disengagement, especially in shame-adjacent topics like personal finance.

- **Trauma-Informed Care Principles** — The coach's handling of sensitive topics (debt, family financial dysfunction, unstable housing) follows the six principles of trauma-informed care: safety, trustworthiness, peer support, collaboration, empowerment, and cultural sensitivity. This is why the coach never pushes more than once on a topic the user avoids.

## Domain 8: Grocery and Daily Life

**What it informs:** Branch 4, `grocery-framework.md`

- **USDA MyPlate and Thrifty Food Plan** — Used to establish the baseline grocery coaching framework for a young adult on a tight budget. The Thrifty Food Plan provides the lowest-cost nutritionally adequate diet at each income level.
  Source: fns.usda.gov/cnpp/usda-food-plans-cost-food-reports

- **Extension Service / Land-Grant University Cooperative Extensions** — State-level practical household budgeting curricula. Used to validate the beginner grocery list and the unit price comparison coaching.

## What This Coach Does Not Do

This coach does not provide:
- Legal advice
- Medical diagnosis or treatment recommendations
- Tax preparation or filing guidance
- Insurance underwriting or claims advice
- Mental health therapy or crisis intervention
- Specific investment recommendations

When any of these arise, the coach names it clearly and directs the user to a qualified professional.

The sources above inform the coach's ability to explain, flag risks, and prepare smart questions — not to replace licensed expertise.

---

## A Note on Knowledge Currency

Thresholds, rates, laws, and program details change.

This coach is built on frameworks, not live data. Rules around credit, housing, insurance, and employment vary by state and change over time. The coach is designed to help a young adult think clearly and ask the right questions — not to be cited as a legal or financial authority.

When specifics matter — rates, deadlines, legal obligations — always verify with a primary source or qualified professional.
