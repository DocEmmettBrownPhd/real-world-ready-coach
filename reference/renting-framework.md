# Renting Framework

Renting an apartment is often a young adult's first major financial commitment. The coach goes deep — not surface level.

---

## Core Principle

Rent is not the only number. The coach helps users calculate the full cost of moving in and living there — before they fall in love with an apartment.

---

## Step 1: Affordability Check

General guideline: rent should not exceed 30% of monthly take-home pay.

Example:
> If take-home is $2,000/month, rent should ideally be $600 or less. At $800, it is tight. At $1,100, there is almost nothing left for food, transportation, and emergencies.

Always calculate:
- Monthly rent
- Utilities (electric, gas, water)
- Internet
- Renter's insurance
- Transportation from that location to work or school
- Any parking fees

---

## Step 2: Move-In Cost Calculation

The full move-in cost is almost always more than first month's rent.

Typically includes:
- First month's rent
- Security deposit (often one to two months)
- Last month's rent (sometimes required)
- Application fee
- Move-in or admin fees
- Pet deposit if applicable

Coach the user to ask for the exact move-in total in writing before applying.

---

## Step 3: Cosigner Coaching (Apartment)

Ask separately from car cosigner conversation:
1. Does your income and credit meet the landlord's requirements?
2. If not, do you have a family member or trusted adult who could cosign?
3. Does the cosigner understand they are responsible if you do not pay?
4. Are you comfortable asking that person?
5. Is there a roommate option instead?

Coaching note: A cosigner for an apartment takes on full legal liability for the lease. Make sure the user understands this before asking someone.

---

## Step 4: Roommate Considerations

If the user cannot afford the apartment alone:
- A roommate splits cost but adds complexity
- Both names on the lease means both are responsible for the full rent
- Discuss what happens if the roommate stops paying
- Recommend a written roommate agreement even between friends
- Ask if the landlord allows roommates and whether both applicants need to qualify

---

## Step 5: Lease Review (Deep)

Do not skim the lease. Go through it section by section.

**Key sections to cover:**
- Lease dates and renewal terms
- Monthly rent, due date, grace period, late fee
- Security deposit amount and return process
- What can cause deposit deductions
- Maintenance and repair responsibilities
- Appliance repair responsibilities
- Utility responsibilities
- Pet policy and fees
- Guest and subletting rules
- Parking rules and fees
- Early termination penalty
- Notice required to vacate
- Landlord access rules
- Any verbal promises — get them in writing

**Lease Red Flags:**
- Blank spaces anywhere in the document
- Tenant responsible for structural or major repairs
- No clear deposit return process
- Automatic renewal without clear opt-out notice requirement
- Pressure to sign the same day
- Requests to pay deposit or rent through unusual methods like wire transfer or apps
- No property management contact information

---

## Step 6: Questions to Ask the Landlord

1. What is the exact total move-in cost?
2. What utilities am I responsible for?
3. Who handles maintenance requests and what is the response time?
4. Who is responsible for appliance repairs?
5. What is the process for getting my security deposit back?
6. What counts as normal wear and tear vs damage?
7. What is the early termination fee?
8. Can I get all verbal promises in writing?
9. Is the unit inspected before move-in and do I get a condition report?

---

## Coaching Script

> Rent is not the only number. Before you fall in love with this apartment, let's figure out the full monthly cost and the full move-in cost. What is your take-home pay, and how much money do you have saved right now?
