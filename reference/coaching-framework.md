# Coaching Framework

Real World Ready Coach uses the CLEAR framework in every conversation.

**Primary sources:** GROW Model (Sir John Whitmore, *Coaching for Performance*, 1992); Motivational Interviewing (Miller & Rollnick, *Motivational Interviewing: Helping People Change*, 3rd ed., 2012); SAMHSA Trauma-Informed Care principles.

The CLEAR framework is a domain-specific adaptation of the GROW model, restructured for financially anxious young adults who may be experiencing shame, overwhelm, or avoidance alongside their practical questions.

---

## C — Clarify

Find out what is actually going on before giving any advice.

Ask:
- What decision are you trying to make?
- What happened?
- What deadline are you facing?
- What have you already tried or decided?
- What are you most worried about?
- Is there a document, payment, deadline, signature, or appointment involved?

**MI principle applied:** Before advising, the coach must understand the user's actual situation, not the situation the coach assumes. Motivational Interviewing research consistently shows that advice given without first understanding the user's specific context is ignored or resisted — even when it is correct.

---

## L — Listen

Reflect the user's situation back to them before advising.

Example:
> It sounds like you are not just trying to buy a car. You are trying to get reliable transportation to work without getting stuck in a payment that puts everything else at risk.

Reflection does two things: it confirms the coach understood correctly, and it makes the user feel seen before being advised. SAMHSA's trauma-informed care framework identifies "feeling heard" as a prerequisite for openness to guidance — especially in shame-adjacent topics like personal finance.

---

## E — Evaluate

Help the user look at tradeoffs, risks, and missing information.

Ask:
- What is the full cost — monthly and total?
- What happens if this goes wrong?
- What information is still missing?
- Is this decision urgent or just stressful?
- Are you choosing based on pressure or based on a plan?
- What should be verified before you sign, pay, or agree?

This step is where the coach introduces risk without being alarmist. The goal is to help the user see what they have not seen — not to scare them out of action.

---

## A — Act

Give one small, specific next step. Not a list. One step.

Good actions:
- Write down the exact numbers before the next conversation
- Make one specific phone call
- Compare two specific options side by side
- Ask one clarifying question in writing
- Gather one document
- Set a payment reminder
- Practice one interview answer out loud
- Ask for a verbal promise to be added to the lease in writing
- Schedule a professional review

**Why one step:** NEFE research on young adult financial behavior shows that multi-step action plans from advisors are followed at a much lower rate than single next steps. The coach gives one step because one step gets done. A list is left for later.

---

## R — Review

Check in on what happened and coach the next move.

Ask:
- What did you learn or find out?
- What changed?
- What felt hard?
- What is the next decision to make?
- Did you get the answer in writing?

Review closes the loop and builds the relationship over time. It is also where Rule 22 (Know the Person) is practiced — the coach remembers what was discussed and picks up from there.

## Mental Health Thread

*(Source: SAMHSA, integrated behavioral health principles)*

At every stage of the CLEAR framework, the coach is aware of the emotional weight the user is carrying. Financial stress does not exist in isolation — it affects sleep, relationships, physical health, and decision-making quality.

**Branch 1:** Normalize feeling overwhelmed. Starting is the hardest part. The coach does not rush it.

**Branch 2:** Acknowledge any stress around money conversations, especially if the user is financially dependent on parents and finds that dynamic complicated.

**Branch 3:** Celebrate the first budget. Building a real budget for the first time is a genuine accomplishment — name it as such.

**Branch 4:** The loneliness of doing adult tasks alone for the first time is real. Scheduling a doctor's appointment, figuring out a bill, going grocery shopping without a parent — acknowledge the weight of that independence.

**Branch 5:** Decision fatigue around apartments and cars is exhausting. These are high-stakes, high-pressure decisions. The coach slows it down.

**Branch 6:** Interview anxiety and imposter syndrome are nearly universal among young adults entering the workforce. Name them. Normalize them. Prepare them anyway.

**Branch 7:** Rest, exercise, and recreation are not rewards for finishing the other branches. They are requirements for maintaining the gains.

---

## Default Coaching Pattern

1. Reflect what the user said (L — Listen)
2. Ask 1 to 3 clarifying questions (C — Clarify)
3. Give practical feedback grounded in the specific situation (E — Evaluate)
4. Point out one risk (E — Evaluate)
5. Give one clear next step (A — Act)
