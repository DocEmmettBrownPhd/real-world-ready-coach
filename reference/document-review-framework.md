# Document Review Framework

The coach helps young adults review important documents before signing, paying, agreeing, or panicking.

**Primary sources:** HUD lease review guidance; CFPB auto loan and credit card disclosure standards; FTC consumer document guidance; DOL offer letter standards; HIPAA patient records rights.

---

## Core Principle

The coach does not replace a qualified professional.

The coach helps the user:
- Understand the document in plain language
- Identify the most important details
- Spot possible red flags
- Notice missing or vague information
- Prepare better questions to ask the other party
- Decide the safest next step

A young adult who understands what they are signing is dramatically less likely to be trapped by terms they did not notice. That is the purpose of this framework.

---

## Default Review Structure (8 Steps)

For every document shared:

1. **What this appears to be** — name the document type
2. **Plain-English summary** — what is the main purpose of this document in 2–3 sentences
3. **Important details** — numbers, dates, fees, deadlines, parties, obligations
4. **Possible red flags** — anything unusual, vague, missing, or potentially harmful
5. **Things that need clarification** — questions to raise with the other party before proceeding
6. **Questions to ask** — specific, practical questions the user can bring to the landlord, dealer, employer, or provider
7. **Professional review recommendation** — if the stakes or complexity warrant it
8. **Safest next step** — one concrete action

---

## Language Rules

**Always say:**
- "This may be a red flag."
- "This needs clarification before you sign."
- "This could cost you money later."
- "Ask for this in writing before you agree."
- "This is worth having a professional review."

**Never say:**
- "This is legal." (The coach is not a lawyer.)
- "This is definitely a scam." (The coach cannot make that determination.)
- "This is safe." (The coach cannot verify safety.)
- "You should sign." (The coach helps evaluate — not decide.)
- "This medical result means you have..." (The coach is not a doctor.)

## Lease Review Checklist

*(Based on HUD tenant rights guidance and Nolo Press lease review standards)*

**Dates and terms:**
- Lease start and end date
- What happens at the end — does it convert to month-to-month or auto-renew for another year?
- Notice required to vacate (typically 30–60 days)

**Financials:**
- Monthly rent and due date
- Grace period before late fees kick in
- Late fee amount (some states cap this)
- Security deposit amount
- Move-in fees separate from the deposit
- Utilities included vs. tenant responsibility (get a written estimate of what utilities typically run)

**Responsibilities:**
- Maintenance and repair — who is responsible for what?
- Appliance repair — if the dishwasher breaks, who fixes it?
- Landlord entry rules — how much notice is required? (Most states require 24 hours minimum)

**Restrictions:**
- Pet policy and any associated deposits or fees
- Guest policy — when does a guest become an unauthorized occupant?
- Subletting rules
- Parking assignment and fees

**Exit terms:**
- Early termination penalty — exact amount or formula
- Security deposit return process — timeline and documentation requirements
- What counts as damage vs. normal wear and tear

**Other:**
- Cosigner terms and liability
- Any verbal promises made during the showing — are they in the written lease?
- Pressure to sign quickly without review time

### Lease Red Flags
- Blank spaces anywhere in the document — every blank should be filled or crossed out before signing
- Tenant responsible for structural or major system repairs
- No clear or specific deposit return process
- Automatic renewal clause with no clear written opt-out
- Payment methods that leave no paper trail (wire transfer, Zelle, Venmo, cash without receipt)
- No physical address or contact information for the property management company
- Terms that differ from what was described verbally during the showing

## Car Loan Review Checklist

*(Based on CFPB auto loan disclosure standards)*

- Loan amount (principal financed)
- APR — is it fixed or variable?
- Loan term in months
- Monthly payment
- Total amount repaid over the life of the loan (monthly payment × term) — always calculate this
- Down payment applied
- Prepayment penalty — is there a fee for paying off early?
- Late payment fee
- Repossession terms

**Add-ons to examine:**
- GAP insurance — is the price reasonable? (Often cheaper through your own insurer)
- Extended warranty — what exactly does it cover and exclude?
- Paint/fabric protection — these are almost always high-margin dealer add-ons with low practical value
- Credit life or disability insurance — often overpriced relative to standalone policies

### Car Loan Red Flags
- APR significantly higher than current market rates (check the CFPB's rate data for reference)
- Loan term of 72 or 84 months on a used vehicle
- Monthly payment presented without total cost or APR
- Add-ons included in the contract that were not discussed
- Prepayment penalty — this prevents refinancing to a lower rate later
- Signed contract terms different from what was verbally agreed

## Job Offer Review Checklist

*(Based on DOL offer letter standards and CFPB financial planning guidance)*

- Job title (confirm it matches what was discussed)
- Pay rate — hourly or salary? Is it the amount discussed?
- Pay schedule — weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly?
- Hours per week — are they guaranteed or variable?
- Overtime rules — eligible for OT or classified as exempt?
- Start date and work location
- Remote, hybrid, or in-person expectations
- Benefits: health, dental, vision, 401(k) and any employer match, paid time off
- Probationary period — are any benefits delayed?
- Required duties — do they match what was described in the interview?
- Noncompete or non-solicitation clause — read this carefully; it can limit future employment
- At-will employment language — standard in most states, but understand what it means
- Background check requirements and timing

### Job Offer Red Flags
- Commission-only compensation with no guaranteed base
- Schedule described as "flexible" with no guaranteed minimum hours
- Job duties significantly broader than described in the interview
- Unpaid training period required before employment begins
- Required expenses (uniform, tools, travel) not reimbursed
- Noncompete clause that restricts working in the same field for 1–2 years after leaving
- Pressure to start before reviewing and signing paperwork

## Medical Test Result Review

*(HIPAA gives patients the right to their records. The coach helps interpret structure, not diagnose.)*

Do not diagnose. Help the user understand the structure and prepare questions.

Look for:
- Test name and collection date
- Result value and the reference range (normal range)
- High (H) or Low (L) flags next to out-of-range results
- Provider notes or comments
- Recommended follow-up or next steps

**Questions to help the user prepare to ask their provider:**
1. Which of these results are outside the normal range?
2. Are any of these results urgent or time-sensitive?
3. Could anything I ate, drank, or took (medications, supplements) affect these results?
4. Do I need a repeat test to confirm any of these findings?
5. What is the recommended next step, and how soon does it need to happen?

**Non-negotiable rule:** If any result is marked CRITICAL or URGENT by the lab — tell the user to contact their provider today, not later.

## Bill Review Checklist

- Who sent the bill — is this a company the user has a relationship with?
- Service or product being charged for — does the user recognize it?
- Account or reference number
- Service date — does it match when the service was received?
- Due date and late fee
- Insurance or financial aid adjustments applied — were they processed correctly?
- Itemized breakdown — are individual charges listed?
- Contact information for questions or disputes

**Coaching advice:** Verify the bill directly using official contact information (from the provider's website — not from the bill itself, which could be fraudulent) before making any payment, especially if anything looks unfamiliar. Medical bills in particular are frequently sent in error or with billing codes that do not match the actual service received.
