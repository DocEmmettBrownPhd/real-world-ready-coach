# Grocery Framework

The goal is not to give the user a perfect meal plan. The goal is to build one they will actually follow.

---

## Core Principle

Eating out every day is one of the fastest ways to lose money without noticing it. The coach helps users replace the most expensive meals first — not overhaul their entire life at once.

---

## Step 1: Assess the Situation

Before recommending anything, ask:
1. What cooking equipment do you have — stove, microwave, air fryer, toaster, fridge?
2. What are three foods you actually like?
3. How many days a week are you currently eating out?
4. What is your rough food budget?
5. Do you have roommates who share food costs?

---

## Step 2: The Simple Grocery List

For a beginner, start with basics that cover most meals:

**Protein:**
- Eggs
- Canned tuna or chicken
- Rotisserie chicken
- Ground beef or turkey
- Beans or lentils

**Carbs:**
- Rice
- Pasta
- Bread
- Tortillas
- Oats

**Vegetables:**
- Frozen vegetables (cheap, easy, no waste)
- Bagged salad
- Whatever is on sale

**Staples:**
- Olive oil or cooking spray
- Salt, pepper, basic seasonings
- Pasta sauce
- Peanut butter
- Canned soup or broth

---

## Step 3: Simple Meal Ideas That Require Minimal Skill

- Scrambled eggs and toast
- Rice and canned beans
- Pasta with jarred sauce and ground beef
- Rotisserie chicken with frozen vegetables
- Peanut butter and banana on bread
- Oatmeal with peanut butter
- Quesadillas with chicken and cheese
- Tuna with crackers
- Soup from a can with added vegetables or rice

---

## Step 4: Cost Comparison Coaching

Help users see the real cost of eating out:

Example:
> A $12 lunch five days a week = $60/week = $240/month.
> A grocery run for $80 can cover most meals for the week.
> That is $160 saved in one month.

Challenge: replace just two eating-out meals per week first. Not all of them.

---

## Step 5: Reducing Food Waste

- Buy what you will actually eat
- Frozen vegetables last longer and are just as nutritious
- Rotisserie chicken can become multiple meals
- Cook once, eat twice — make extra portions
- Keep backup meals for busy days (oatmeal, canned soup, eggs)

---

## Coaching Script

> I do not want to give you a meal plan you will quit in three days. I want to find the two or three meals you like, that you can actually cook with what you have, and that replace the most expensive eating-out habit. What do you actually like to eat?
