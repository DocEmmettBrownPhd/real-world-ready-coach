# Banking and Credit Framework

The goal is to help young adults enter the banking and credit system safely — without getting trapped by fees, overdrafts, debt, or bad habits.

---

## Part 1: Opening a Bank Account

### Account Types
- **Checking:** For spending, bills, debit card, direct deposit
- **Savings:** For storing money, emergency fund, short-term goals
- **Credit union account:** Often lower fees, local service
- **Online bank account:** Often fewer fees, no physical branch

### What to Compare
- Monthly maintenance fee
- Minimum balance requirement
- Overdraft fee and overdraft protection options
- ATM fee and ATM network access
- Direct deposit options
- Mobile app quality
- Account opening requirements

### Red Flags
- Monthly fees that cannot be waived
- High overdraft fees with automatic overdraft enrollment
- Minimum balance requirements the user cannot maintain
- Confusing fee structures
- Pressure to add products they do not need

### Coaching Script
> The goal is to find an account that lets you get paid, pay bills, and avoid fees. Do you need a physical branch, or are you comfortable with online banking?

## Part 2: Understanding Credit

### Simple Explanation
Credit is your track record of borrowing money and paying it back. A credit score is the number lenders use to decide how risky it is to lend to you.

### What Credit Affects
- Car loan approvals and interest rates
- Apartment applications
- Credit card approvals
- Some utility deposits
- Some insurance pricing
- Some employment background checks

### The Five Main Factors
1. **Payment history** — Do you pay on time?
2. **Credit utilization** — How much of your available credit are you using?
3. **Length of credit history** — How long have your accounts been open?
4. **Credit mix** — Do you have different types of accounts?
5. **New credit inquiries** — Are you applying for a lot of credit at once?

### Beginner Credit Rules
- Pay every bill on time — this is the most important factor
- Keep balances well below the credit limit
- Do not apply for multiple cards at once
- Check credit reports for errors once a year
- Set up autopay or payment reminders
- Treat credit as a tool, not as spending money

## Part 3: First Credit Card

### Safe First-Card Options
- Student credit card
- Secured credit card
- Credit union starter card
- No-annual-fee beginner card
- Authorized user on a responsible adult's account

### First Card Habit Plan
1. Pick one small monthly use (gas, groceries, phone bill)
2. Keep the balance low
3. Set a due-date reminder
4. Pay the full balance every month
5. Never spend money you do not already have
6. Review the statement monthly

### Red Flags
- Annual fees on a first card
- High APR with no plan to pay in full
- Store cards opened at checkout under pressure
- Using the card for lifestyle spending beyond the budget
- Cash advances
- Paying only the minimum indefinitely

### Coaching Script
> I want you to build credit without building debt. The safest first-card habit is to put one small planned expense on the card, then pay it off in full every month. What would you use it for, and how would you make sure it gets paid?

## Part 4: Credit Score Coaching

### No Credit History
> No credit is not the same as bad credit. It just means lenders do not have enough history to judge yet.

Next steps: secured card, student card, or becoming an authorized user. Use lightly. Pay on time. Wait for history to build.

### Bad Credit
> Bad credit can be improved — but the first job is to stop new damage.

Next steps: catch up on late payments, lower card balances, avoid new unnecessary applications, dispute errors, create payment reminders.

### Wants a Higher Score Fast
> Be careful with anyone promising a quick credit fix. Real improvement comes from consistent behavior over time.
