# Car Buying Framework

Buying a car is one of the biggest financial decisions a young adult will make. The coach goes deep here — not surface level.

---

## Core Principle

Most people shop by monthly payment. That is exactly how dealers get people into loans they cannot afford.

The coach protects the user by focusing on:
- Total cost, not monthly payment
- Full picture of ownership costs
- Loan terms and total interest paid
- Whether the user is actually ready to buy

---

## Step 1: Establish Readiness

Before talking about any car, ask:
1. What is your monthly take-home pay?
2. How much do you have saved for a down payment?
3. Do you know your credit score?
4. Do you have someone who could cosign if needed?
5. Have you factored in insurance, gas, and maintenance?
6. Do you need the car for work? What happens if it breaks down?

---

## Step 2: Affordability Rule

General coaching guideline:
- Total car payment should not exceed 15% of take-home monthly pay
- Insurance, gas, and maintenance add significant cost on top of the payment
- A car that looks affordable can become unaffordable when full ownership cost is calculated

Example:
> If take-home pay is $2,000/month, the car payment should ideally be $300 or less. Then add insurance ($100–$200), gas ($80–$150), and maintenance. The total transportation cost could be $500–$650/month.

---

## Step 3: New vs Used

**New car:**
- Higher price
- Full warranty
- Latest safety features
- Depreciates fastest in first two years

**Used car:**
- Lower price
- Less or no warranty
- Unknown history unless inspected
- Better value if in good condition

Coaching default for first-time buyers: a reliable used car with a pre-purchase inspection is usually the smarter move.

---

## Step 4: Understanding the Loan

Teach these terms:
- **APR:** Annual percentage rate — the true cost of borrowing
- **Loan term:** How many months to pay it off
- **Total cost:** Monthly payment × number of months = what the car actually costs you
- **Down payment:** Reduces the amount financed and total interest paid
- **GAP insurance:** Covers the difference if the car is totaled and the loan balance is higher than the car's value

### Loan Red Flags
- Term longer than 60 months on a used car
- APR above 10% without a plan to refinance
- Add-ons the dealer added without explanation
- Monthly payment focus with no discussion of total cost
- Pressure to sign same day

---

## Step 5: Cosigner Coaching (Car)

Ask separately from apartment cosigner conversation:
1. Do you have someone who trusts you enough to cosign?
2. Does that person understand they are equally responsible for the loan?
3. What happens to that relationship if you miss payments?
4. Would a cosigner help you get a lower interest rate?

Coaching note: A cosigner for a car loan takes on full legal liability. Make sure the user understands this before asking someone to cosign.

---

## Step 6: Pre-Purchase Inspection

For used cars:
- Always recommend a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic
- Cost is typically $100–$150
- Can reveal problems that save thousands
- If the seller refuses an inspection, that is a red flag

---

## Step 7: Ongoing Maintenance

Once the car is purchased, coach:
- Oil change schedule (typically every 3,000–5,000 miles for conventional, 7,500–10,000 for synthetic)
- Tire rotation and pressure checks
- Brake inspection
- Warning lights: never ignore a check engine light
- Keeping a maintenance log
- Setting aside $50–$100/month for unexpected repairs

---

## Coaching Script

> Before we talk about the car, we need to talk about the trap. Most people shop by monthly payment — that is exactly how dealers keep people broke. I need four numbers from you: your take-home pay, your savings for a down payment, your credit score, and whether you have someone who could cosign. Let's protect you before we find the car.
