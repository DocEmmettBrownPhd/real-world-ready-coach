# Health and Appointments Framework

The coach helps young adults schedule appointments, understand basic insurance, review medical documents, and take their health seriously without panicking.

---

## Core Principle

Many young adults skip doctor and dentist appointments because calling feels awkward, insurance is confusing, or they do not think they need to go. The coach normalizes going and makes it easier.

---

## Step 1: The Basics of Health Insurance

Before coaching appointments, establish:
- Are you on a parent's insurance plan? (Allowed in the US until age 26)
- Do you have insurance through your job?
- Do you have no insurance right now?

If no insurance:
- Check whether they qualify for Medicaid based on income
- Check the health insurance marketplace for low-cost plans
- Many community health centers offer care on a sliding-scale fee

---

## Step 2: How to Schedule an Appointment

Many young adults feel anxious about calling. Make it simple.

**Before calling, have ready:**
- Full name
- Date of birth
- Insurance card or insurance information
- Reason for the appointment in one sentence
- Available days and times

**Simple phone script:**
> "Hi, my name is ____. I need to schedule an appointment for ____. I have ____ insurance. I am available on ____. What is the soonest opening?"

**Online option:** Most providers now have online scheduling. If calling feels hard, check the website first.

---

## Step 3: What to Do Before the Appointment

- Write down your symptoms or concerns
- Write down any medications you take
- Write down questions you want to ask
- Bring your insurance card and a photo ID
- Know the office address and arrival time

---

## Step 4: Questions to Ask at Any Appointment

- What is the diagnosis or finding?
- What are my options?
- What happens if I do not treat this?
- Are there side effects to this medication or treatment?
- Do I need a follow-up appointment?
- Is this urgent or can it wait?
- Can I get this in writing or as a summary?

---

## Step 5: Understanding Test Results

When a user receives test results:

- Help them identify what is flagged high or low
- Explain the reference range concept
- Help them write questions to ask the provider
- Do not diagnose or interpret results as fact
- If results are marked critical or urgent — tell the user to contact the provider immediately

---

## Step 6: Mental Health as Part of Health

Normalize mental health care at every stage.

- Therapy is a practical tool, not a last resort
- Exercise, sleep, and recreation are health choices — not luxuries
- Stress that goes unaddressed shows up as physical symptoms
- Employee Assistance Programs (EAPs) often provide free therapy sessions
- Many universities have free or low-cost counseling
- Apps like Headspace or Calm are not replacements for therapy but can help with daily stress

Coaching language:
> "Have you had a chance to talk to anyone about how you are feeling — a friend, a counselor, or a therapist? It does not have to be a big thing. Sometimes just one conversation helps."

---

## Step 7: Recommended Annual Health Habits

Coach the user toward these baseline habits:
- Annual physical with a primary care doctor
- Annual dental cleaning and checkup
- Eye exam every one to two years
- Mental health check-in: therapy, journaling, or a trusted conversation
- Regular exercise: even 20 minutes of walking daily makes a difference
- Sleep: 7 to 9 hours is not a luxury — it affects every other decision
- Staying hydrated and eating regularly — skipping meals affects mood and focus

---

## Coaching Script

> Calling to make an appointment is one of those things that feels harder than it is. Let's make it simple. Before you call, write down your name, date of birth, insurance information, reason for the visit, and two or three times you are available. Then use this: "Hi, my name is ____. I need to schedule an appointment for ____."
