# Adulting Milestones

This file lists the core milestones Real World Ready Coach helps users work through, organized by branch. Each milestone is grounded in the domain frameworks in this reference folder.

**Sources:** CFPB financial capability research; NEFE young adult financial survey data; AAP Bright Futures transition guidelines; DOL youth employment data; HUD housing counseling standards.

---

## Branch 1 — Still in the Nest

- Recognizing the need for a plan — naming what feels overwhelming without judgment
- Identifying what area of life feels most urgent or most scary
- Understanding that not knowing where to start is normal — NEFE research shows fewer than 20% of high school graduates receive formal personal finance instruction before leaving home
- First conversation with the coach: what do you know, what do you not know, what do you want to change?

---

## Branch 2 — Money In

- Identifying the income source: parents, allowance, part-time job, or first career job
- Understanding gross pay vs. take-home pay — taxes, Social Security, Medicare, and any deductions
- Reading a pay stub for the first time — identifying every line
- Understanding pay schedules: weekly (52 checks/year), biweekly (26 checks/year), semi-monthly (24 checks/year), monthly (12 checks/year)
- Knowing when money arrives and building spending decisions around the real deposit date
- For job-based income: understanding at-will employment and what it means for stability

---

## Branch 3 — Money Managed

*(FDIC Money Smart curriculum; CFPB financial tools)*

- Opening a checking account — comparing options, avoiding fee traps
- Opening a savings account — physically separate from checking
- Understanding bank fees: monthly maintenance, overdraft, ATM, minimum balance
- Setting up direct deposit
- Building a first budget based on verified take-home pay — using the 6-priority order from budget-framework.md
- Separating needs (housing, food, transportation) from wants (entertainment, dining out, extras)
- Avoiding overdrafts — setting up alerts, tracking balance
- Starting an emergency fund — even $20 per paycheck
- Setting up payment reminders or autopay for recurring bills

---

## Branch 4 — Daily Independence

*(USDA Thrifty Food Plan; HealthCare.gov; CFPB credit card guidance)*

- Building a basic grocery list around budget and available equipment — see grocery-framework.md
- Planning simple repeatable meals that prevent defaulting to eating out
- Reducing eating out — replacing one or two meals per week first, not all of them
- Comparing cost per meal at home vs. eating out
- Scheduling a doctor or dentist appointment independently for the first time
- Understanding an insurance card: member ID, group number, copay, deductible, in-network vs. out-of-network
- Reading and questioning a bill before paying — see document-review-framework.md
- Applying for a first credit card responsibly — secured card or student card first
- Understanding the five FICO score factors and how a first card affects them

---

## Branch 5 — Bigger Decisions

*(HUD housing counseling; CFPB auto loan guidance; myFICO credit education; Nolo Press)*

- Understanding rent affordability — rent should not exceed 30% of take-home pay
- Calculating the full move-in cost — not just first month's rent
- Understanding cosigners for apartments — liability, relationship, and alternatives
- Roommate considerations — joint liability, written agreements, landlord approval
- Reviewing an apartment lease section by section — see renting-framework.md
- Asking landlord questions before signing — full checklist in renting-framework.md
- Buying a car: establishing readiness before looking at any vehicle
- Car affordability — total cost vs. monthly payment, full ownership cost
- Car cosigners — handled separately from apartment cosigner conversation
- Understanding APR, loan term, and total repayment amount
- Getting a pre-purchase inspection on any used vehicle
- Understanding credit scores and reports — checking at AnnualCreditReport.com (free, federally mandated)
- Disputing errors on a credit report
- Understanding what improves a credit score over time vs. what hurts it

---

## Branch 6 — Career Ready

*(STAR method; DOL career guidance; DOL Youth Employment)*

- Preparing for job interviews — knowing the company, the role, and the three core questions
- Practicing STAR method answers to behavioral questions
- Following up after interviews — email within 24 hours
- Reviewing a job offer before accepting — never accept on the spot
- Understanding pay, schedule, benefits, and noncompete clauses in writing
- Understanding at-will employment
- Moving from a first job to a career-level job — skills gained, resume updated, references identified
- Reviewing every important document before signing — using document-review-framework.md
- Knowing when to call a professional: lawyer, financial advisor, HR specialist

---

## Branch 7 — Soaring

*(CFPB emergency fund research; SAMHSA wellness principles; AAP Bright Futures)*

- Emergency savings reaching 1 month of expenses, then 3 months — the CFPB benchmark
- Budget working consistently for 3+ months without major breakdowns
- Credit score building on track — on-time payments, low utilization
- Regular health and dental checkups scheduled proactively, not reactively
- Decisions made from facts and plans, not pressure or panic
- Exercise, rest, recreation, and mental health as ongoing habits — not rewards for finishing everything else
- Knowing when and how to ask for professional help: accountant, attorney, financial planner
- Awareness of next-level financial goals: retirement contributions, investing basics, debt payoff strategies

## Coaching Priority When Overwhelmed

When a user does not know where to start, coach the most urgent milestone first.

This priority order follows CFPB emergency financial coaching protocols, which sequence coaching by immediate survival need rather than financial optimality:

1. **Safety** — Is the user in an unsafe housing or personal situation?
2. **Housing stability** — Is there a risk of eviction or homelessness?
3. **Food** — Is the user eating consistently?
4. **Transportation to work or school** — Can they get to income or education?
5. **Income** — Is there money coming in?
6. **Banking access** — Can they receive and manage money safely?
7. **Bills and debt** — What is at risk of going to collections?
8. **Credit protection** — Is credit being actively damaged?
9. **Document review** — Is there a deadline to sign something?
10. **Long-term planning** — Everything else, once the urgent is handled
