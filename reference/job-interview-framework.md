# Job Interview Framework

The coach helps young adults prepare for interviews — from first fast food job to first career-level position.

---

## Core Principle

Preparation kills nervousness. The coach turns nervous energy into practice.

---

## Step 1: Understand the Job

Before coaching interview answers, ask:
1. What job is the interview for?
2. Is this a first job or a career step?
3. What do you know about the company?
4. Have you looked at the job description?
5. What do you think they are looking for?

---

## Step 2: The Core Three Questions

Every interview has some version of these three. Practice them first.

**Question 1: Tell me about yourself.**
Coach the user to:
- Keep it under 90 seconds
- Start with where they are now
- Add one relevant experience or strength
- End with why they want this job

**Question 2: Why do you want this job?**
Coach the user to:
- Be specific — not "I need money"
- Connect something about the company to something they care about
- Sound genuine, not scripted

**Question 3: Tell me about a time you handled a problem.**
Use the STAR method:
- **S**ituation — what was the context
- **T**ask — what needed to be done
- **A**ction — what you specifically did
- **R**esult — what happened because of it

---

## Step 3: Common First-Job Questions

- Are you available on weekends and holidays?
- Can you work a flexible schedule?
- Have you worked in a team before?
- How do you handle a difficult customer or coworker?
- What is your greatest strength?
- What would you say is an area you are working on?

---

## Step 4: Questions the User Should Ask

Coach every user to have at least two questions ready.

Good questions:
- What does a typical day look like in this role?
- What do the best people in this position do differently?
- How is performance reviewed?
- What does growth look like from this position?
- What is the team culture like?

Questions to avoid:
- How much do I make? (wait until they offer or ask)
- How soon can I get promoted?
- How many sick days do I get?

---

## Step 5: Reviewing the Job Offer

When a user receives an offer, coach them to review before accepting.

Check:
- Pay rate — is it what was discussed?
- Pay schedule
- Hours and whether they are guaranteed
- Overtime rules
- Benefits: health, dental, vision, retirement
- Start date
- Probation period
- Any noncompete or non-solicitation clauses
- At-will employment language

Red flags:
- Commission-only with no guaranteed base
- Duties much broader than discussed
- Unpaid training
- Noncompete clause that would prevent future employment in the field
- Pressure to start before reviewing paperwork

---

## Step 6: Moving from First Job to Career Job

Coach the user to think about:
- What skills did this first job build?
- What is the next step — more responsibility, a different field, more education?
- Is the resume updated with this experience?
- Are there references from this job?
- What do they want from the next job that this one did not have?

---

## Coaching Script

> Nervous is normal. We are going to turn that into preparation. Tell me what the job is and then answer this like you are in the interview right now: "Tell me about yourself." I will coach your answer and make it stronger.
